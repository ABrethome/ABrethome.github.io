Here's what caught my attention this month; a mix of practical insights, emerging patterns, and things worth learning.

---

**Title: Beyond Point Predictions: A Practical Introduction to Bayesian Neural Networks**

[Link](https://towardsdatascience.com/beyond-point-predictions-a-practical-introduction-to-bayesian-neural-networks/)

Summary: This hands-on tutorial explains why point-estimate neural nets hide uncertainty and how Bayesian neural networks model weight distributions instead. It walks through variational inference, prior choices, KL/ELBO tradeoffs, and uncertainty calibration on the California Housing dataset. The real value is operational: BNNs make it easier to decide when to trust a prediction, defer it, or escalate for review.

---

**Title: A Practical Guide to AWS for Machine Learning (As a Beginner)**

[Link](https://medium.com/@marinawyss/a-practical-guide-to-aws-for-machine-learning-as-a-beginner-490b975cfd95)

Summary: This is a pragmatic AWS primer for ML newcomers, focusing on the few skills that matter most: IAM, billing, storage, data pipelines, training, deployment, and monitoring. It argues that AWS learning should be skills-first, not service-first, and helps readers build a mental map for how the main services fit together in a production ML stack without getting lost in tool sprawl.

---

**Title: We Pinned Our Model Version to Stay Safe. The Provider Deprecated It Anyway.**

[Link](https://towardsdatascience.com/we-pinned-our-model-version-to-stay-safe-the-provider-deprecated-it-anyway/)

Summary: This article reframes production AI as a lifecycle problem: pinning a model version protects against silent drift, but providers still deprecate models on their own schedule. The key lesson is the re-qualification tax—the eval reruns, prompt retuning, traffic diffing, and canary checks required to prove a replacement is safe. Pinning buys planning time, not immunity.

---

**Title: When Does Graph RAG Actually Add Value? A Hands-On Experiment**

[Link](https://towardsdatascience.com/when-does-graph-rag-actually-add-value-a-hands-on-experiment/)

Summary: This experiment compares plain RAG, graph-only retrieval, hybrid graph + text retrieval, and full-context frontier models on a small corpus. The conclusion is nuanced: graph RAG shines for multi-hop reasoning and provenance, but it offers little advantage when the main challenge is broad retrieval coverage or the corpus fits comfortably in context. The article is a useful reality check against graph RAG hype.

---

**Title: AI Agent Best Practices: Production-Ready Harness Engineering (2026 Guide)**

[Link](https://medium.com/@tort_mario/ai-agent-best-practices-production-ready-harness-engineering-2026-guide-c1236d713fac)

Summary: This article argues that the hard part of agentic AI is not the model—it is the harness: validation, permissions, context layering, tool budgets, observability, and evals. It presents a provider-neutral blueprint for building safer agents, anchored in the open-source agents-best-practices skill, and emphasizes the idea that models propose actions while the runtime enforces policy and execution boundaries.

---

**Title: agents-best-practices**

[Link](https://github.com/DenisSergeevitch/agents-best-practices)

Summary: This repository is a practical playbook for building reliable agent systems, not just prompting better models. It organizes production agent design around a harness: typed tools, permission checks, budgets, observability, durable sessions, and security evals. It is especially useful as a checklist for turning AI experiments into governed, auditable workflows.

---

**Title: GraphRAG with TypeSafe Jev: A System One Approach to Scalable Knowledge Graphs**

[Link](https://towardsdatascience.com/graphrag-with-typesafe-jev-a-system-one-approach-to-scalable-knowledge-graphs/)

Summary: This article proposes a hybrid architecture that separates System 1 micro-decisions from System 2 generative reasoning. In practice, a fast calibrated decision model handles entity resolution, relationship checks, and subgraph pruning while the LLM focuses on final synthesis. The article is a strong argument that GraphRAG can be made more scalable and cheaper when the graph is managed by specialized, low-latency decision engines.

---

**Title: How to Catch Data Drift When Every Feature Looks Normal**

[Link](https://towardsdatascience.com/how-to-catch-data-drift-when-every-feature-looks-normal/)

Summary: This article makes a practical and important point: monitoring each feature individually misses drift in their joint relationships. It introduces adversarial validation with a classifier trained to distinguish reference data from new data, helping catch hidden changes in feature interactions and leakage. The key takeaway is that drift detection should look beyond univariate distributions to the structure of the data itself.
