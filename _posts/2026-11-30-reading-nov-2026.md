Here's what caught my attention this month; a mix of practical insights, emerging patterns, and things worth learning.

---

**Title: The Enterprise Context Layer: A Practitioner’s Guide to 6 Advanced Architectural Patterns**

[Link](https://towardsdatascience.com/the-enterprise-context-layer-a-practitioners-guide-to-6-advanced-architectural-patterns)

Summary: Enterprise agents need more than large context windows: they need relevant, governed context assembled from interconnected systems. This guide presents six patterns for doing that, including graph-based impact analysis, identity and policy enrichment, context serialization, caching, compaction, and sharing task-specific state across agents.

---

**Title: The Agent Permission Brief**

[Link](https://links.minglabs.com/CL0/https:%2F%2Fdrive.google.com%2Fuc%3Fid=1JGA4EhRAmS0Vf0e5a4kAD-lTC81vm8s8%26export=download/1/010201a1a44a66f2-6ba9c226-97af-4423-904f-96bc51790091-000000/SbD5Uv4sMtNCf16eDHtuXdWKx1riuFaP8lpX61fhcJ4=452)

Summary: This two-page worksheet helps teams prepare an AI agent for IT, security, and legal review by defining its job, data access, actions, owner, logs, and off switch. It also proposes four autonomy levels, with evidence-based criteria for increasing an agent’s authority gradually while keeping actions auditable.

---

**Title: Ask AI Output in ASD-STE100 Format and Not in Text Format**

[Link](https://medium.com/neuralnotions/andrej-karpathys-new-suggestion-ask-ai-output-in-asd-ste100-format-and-not-in-text-format-b7b753f52f32)

Summary: The article discusses Karpathy's suggestion to request AI responses in ASD-STE100, a controlled form of technical English, instead of unconstrained prose. Its standardized vocabulary and writing rules aim to make outputs simpler, clearer, and less ambiguous; an approach worth considering when readability and consistency matter.

---

**Title: How to Measure AI Adoption, Performance, and ROI with 12 KPIs**

[Link](https://towardsdatascience.com/how-to-measure-ai-adoption-performance-and-roi-with-12-kpis/)

Summary: AI success needs measurement at three levels: adoption, operational performance, and financial value. The article groups 12 KPIs across those levels and stresses establishing baselines, setting targets, and assigning owners. It also cautions that time saved is not automatically realized savings; ROI should distinguish verified outcomes from estimates.
